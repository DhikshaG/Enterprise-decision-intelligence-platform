# User Research Plan

Preview of why this is required: User research is like asking the people who use the product what problems they actually face, instead of guessing.
It helps us find the real problem before we decide what to build.

This is required even though we have created the problem statement. The Problem statement is our best guess, but this step helps us understand better what really user wants before building a solution - so the solution becomes aligned to the user needs rather than building that on our assumptions

---

## 1. Objective

Determine whether gaps in cross-functional context materially affect business decision-making, identify where those gaps occur, and establish the highest-value problem for the Enterprise Decision Intelligence Platform to address.

---

## 2. Decision to Be Informed

The research will determine:

> **Which cross-functional decision-making problem is sufficiently material, frequent, and underserved to warrant an MVP?**

This prevents research from becoming information gathering for its own sake.

---

## 3. Problem Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | What We Need to Establish |
|---|---|
| **H1 — Information Fragmentation** | Critical decision information is distributed across systems, teams, and formats. |
| **H2 — Context Gap** | Decision-makers cannot efficiently connect events, dependencies, and information across functions. |
| **H3 — Root-Cause Complexity** | Determining why an issue occurred requires significant manual investigation or cross-functional coordination. |
| **H4 — Accountability Gap** | Ownership is difficult to establish when issues span multiple functions. |
| **H5 — Impact Blind Spot** | Downstream operational and business impact is difficult to determine at the point of decision. |
| **H6 — Decision Consequence** | These gaps materially affect decision speed, decision quality, or business outcomes. |

---

## 4. Research Questions

### Decision Context
- What decisions require information from multiple functions?
- How is that information assembled today?
- What makes these decisions difficult?

### Information & Dependencies
- Which systems, teams, and data sources are involved?
- How are relationships between functions identified?
- Where does information become fragmented or lost?

### Issue Resolution
- When an issue occurs, how is it investigated?
- How is the root cause established?
- How is accountability determined?
- How is downstream impact assessed?

### Business Impact
- What happens when the required context is unavailable or delayed?
- What are the consequences in terms of time, cost, risk, quality, or customer impact?

### Existing Approach
- Which tools, reports, meetings, workflows, or manual processes are currently used?
- What works well?
- Where do current approaches fall short?

---

## 5. Target Respondents

### Primary
- Business / Operations Heads
- Functional Heads
- Senior Managers
- Cross-functional Decision-makers

### Secondary
- Business Analysts
- Process Analysts
- Data / BI Analysts
- Digital Transformation Teams
- Functional Managers

Respondents should be selected based on their **involvement in cross-functional decisions**, not job title alone.

---

## 6. Research Design

### Primary Research
**Semi-structured interviews**

Focus on:
- Recent real-world decisions
- Actual incidents
- Existing workflows
- Information sources
- Time spent
- Escalations
- Outcomes

### Secondary Research
**Desk research**

Review:
- Industry research
- Enterprise technology landscape
- Public case studies
- Existing solutions
- Relevant process / decision intelligence literature

---

## 7. Interview Approach

### Principle 1 — Start With Experience
Ask respondents to describe a recent real situation rather than a hypothetical scenario.

### Principle 2 — Follow the Evidence
Probe for:
- What happened?
- Who was involved?
- What information was available?
- What was missing?
- What was done next?
- What was the outcome?

### Principle 3 — Avoid Solution Bias
Do not introduce the Enterprise Decision Intelligence Platform or its proposed capabilities during initial discovery.

### Principle 4 — Test, Don't Confirm
Actively seek evidence that disproves our hypotheses.

### Principle 5 — Separate Fact From Interpretation
Record observed behavior separately from respondent opinions and our interpretation.

---

## 8. Evidence Framework

For every significant finding, capture:

**Situation → Evidence → Friction → Consequence → Existing Workaround**

Example:

> **Situation:** Production disruption  
> **Evidence:** Information required from Production, Maintenance and Procurement  
> **Friction:** Data consolidated manually across systems  
> **Consequence:** Investigation delayed by several hours  
> **Workaround:** Cross-functional calls + spreadsheets

This converts interviews into evidence rather than anecdotes.

---

## 9. Hypothesis Assessment

Each hypothesis will be classified as:

- **Validated**
- **Partially Validated**
- **Not Validated**
- **Disproved**
- **Insufficient Evidence**

No hypothesis will be treated as fact until supported by evidence.

---

## 10. Research Success Criteria

Research should enable us to establish:

1. **Who** experiences the problem.
2. **Which decisions** are affected.
3. **Where** the process breaks down.
4. **How frequently** the problem occurs.
5. **How severe** the consequences are.
6. **What** organizations do today.
7. **Why** current approaches are insufficient.
8. **Which problem** deserves MVP priority.
