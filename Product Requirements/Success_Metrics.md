### Success Metrics

> **Measure whether the MVP improves the intended user and business outcome — not simply whether the product was built.**

---

## Measurement Principle

> **We should not measure whether we built the product; we should measure whether the product achieved the intended user and business outcome.**

---

## 1. North Star Outcome

> **Enable decision-makers to reach an informed understanding of a significant business event with less information-assembly effort.**

---

## 2. Primary Metrics

| Metric | What It Measures | Success Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Time to Understanding** | Time required to understand the event and its context | ↓ Lower |
| **Context Completeness** | Relevant cause, ownership, impact and history identified | ↑ Higher |
| **Decision Readiness** | User's confidence in determining the next step | ↑ Higher |
| **Information-Assembly Effort** | Manual effort required to gather relevant context | ↓ Lower |

---

## 3. Supporting Metrics

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Task Completion Rate** | Can users complete the investigation? |
| **Context Discovery Rate** | Can users find the relevant connections? |
| **Repeat Usage Intent** | Would users use the experience again for similar events? |

---

## 4. MVP Success Threshold

The MVP demonstrates value if users can:

> **Understand → Connect → Assess → Decide**

with **less effort and clearer context** than their current information-gathering approach.

---

## 5. Measurement Approach

### Baseline

Measure the current approach to investigating a comparable business event.

### MVP

Measure the same task using the Enterprise Decision Intelligence Platform.

### Compare

**Time ↓**  
**Effort ↓**  
**Context Completeness ↑**  
**Decision Readiness ↑**

---

## 6. Guardrail

> **Speed alone is not success.**

A faster decision is valuable only if the user reaches an **adequately informed conclusion**.
